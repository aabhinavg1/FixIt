# Sorting

Sorting means arranging data by a rule.

## In one line

Sorting puts things in the order you want.

## Real-life example

An exam dashboard may sort students by marks.

A shopping app may sort products by price.

## Study use case

You can sort:

- topics by priority,
- flashcards by difficulty,
- revision sessions by date,
- or practice questions by score.

## Why it matters

Sorting can make later work easier.

Once data is ordered, it may be easier to search, compare, group, or review.

## What beginners should remember

- Sorting is about order.
- The rule matters.
- Sometimes sorting is the main task, and sometimes it is a step that helps later work.

## Common mistake

Do not sort just because you can.

If you only need the most urgent item, sorting everything may be more work than necessary.

## Try this

Take three lists and sort them by different rules:

- by alphabet,
- by score,
- by deadline.

Notice how the same items feel different when the order changes.
